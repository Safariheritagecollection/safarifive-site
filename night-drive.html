A night drive

Africa after dark, with a sundowner first.

What it is

Every great night drive starts with its opening act: the sundowner. The vehicle stops somewhere with a view, drinks come out, and you watch the sun go down over the bush. Then the spotlight comes on and the drive carries on into the dark, into the hours when the bush changes shift. Many national parks do not allow night drives, so where they are allowed, they are a privilege.

Why it matters

The animals of the night are not the animals of the morning: a leopard leaving to hunt, genets and civets, porcupines, hyenas at work, hippos grazing far from the river, and small wonders like a chameleon glowing pale green in the spotlight. And above it all, a sky with almost no light for a hundred miles. Many guests say the night drive was when they finally understood what "wild place" means.

The sundowner is a given. We do not count it as one of the five, because every safari worth the name has one: a drink in hand, chairs in the river or on a rise with a view, and the sun going down over the bush. On the Safari Five, it is the opening act of the night drive.

Where to find it

Zambia's national parks allow night drives, as do many private concessions in Botswana, Zimbabwe and South Africa. Most national parks in Kenya and Tanzania do not.

What counts

  • Opens with a sundowner as the sun goes down
  • A spotlit drive after dark, with a guide
  • In an area where night drives are permitted
  • Long enough to settle in: an hour or more after sunset

Tips

  • Check before you book: many national parks do not allow night drives
  • Bring a warm layer: nights in the dry season are cool
  • Let your eyes adjust and look for eyeshine
